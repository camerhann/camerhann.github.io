The borough of Waverley, encompassing the charming market towns of Farnham, Godalming, Haslemere, and the large village of Cranleigh, is one of Surrey's most desirable locations. Its picturesque landscapes are shaped by the **River Wey** and its tributaries, which, while creating beautiful scenery, also pose a significant **flood risk** to properties and new developments.

For developers and homeowners in Waverley, understanding the nuances of the local planning process and the specific flood risks associated with the Wey Valley is paramount. Successfully navigating the requirements of **Waverley Borough Council** requires careful consideration and expert guidance. The council's planning approach is underpinned by its [Strategic Flood Risk Assessment (SFRA)](https://www.waverley.gov.uk/Portals/0/Documents/services/planning-and-building/planning-strategies-and-policies/local-plan/WBC%20Level%201%20SFRA%20Report-Appendix%20A%20and%20Figures%201-2.pdf?ver=o_qbW3wg84MwsLz9OcS6ng%3d%3d), which demands a rigorous, evidence-based approach to any development in flood-prone areas. ## Key Challenges for Development in Waverley

1.  **Fluvial Flood Risk from the River Wey:**
    *   The River Wey can respond quickly to heavy rainfall, leading to flooding in well-known areas of Farnham and Godalming, but also impacting smaller tributaries throughout the borough.
    *   The Environment Agency’s flood maps are a starting point, but a site-specific assessment is crucial to understand the true risk.

2.  **Surface Water and Drainage Issues:**
    *   Areas of Cranleigh and other parts of the borough are susceptible to [surface water flooding](/blog/surface-water-flooding-hidden-menace-prepare), where local drainage networks can be overwhelmed by intense downpours. This is a key consideration for new housing developments and extensions.

3.  **Groundwater Vulnerability:**
    *   The geology of the area, including parts of the Greensand Ridge, can lead to high [groundwater levels](/blog/groundwater-flooding-risk-from-below), posing a risk to basements and foundation designs, particularly in lower-lying areas.

4.  **Waverley Borough Council’s Planning Policies:**
    *   The Council places significant emphasis on managing flood risk, as detailed in [Policy CC4 of its Local Plan](https://www.waverley.gov.uk/portals/0/documents/services/planning-and-building/appeal%20inquiries/midhurst_scotland_park/core_documents/cd6/cc4.pdf?ver=381CZdtj_x9ctYJwqJuNMA%3D%3D&timestamp=1701701976906). Their SFRA requires a detailed, site-specific Flood Risk Assessment (FRA) for any development proposed in Flood Zones 2 or 3, or for sites larger than 1 hectare in Flood Zone 1.
    *   Demonstrating that a development is safe, won't increase flood risk elsewhere, and incorporates sustainable drainage is a non-negotiable part of the [planning process](/blog/decoding-development-homeowners-guide-planning-procedures). The council's [evidence base](https://www.waverley.gov.uk/services/planning-and-building/planning-strategies-and-policies/local-plan/evidence-and-supporting-documents) provides further context.

5.  **Conservation and Heritage:**
    *   Waverley is rich in history, with numerous conservation areas and listed buildings, especially in Farnham and Godalming. Any flood mitigation or drainage strategy must be sensitive to this historic character, requiring a nuanced and expert approach.
