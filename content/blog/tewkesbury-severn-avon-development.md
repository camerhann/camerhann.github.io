Tewkesbury's unique location at the confluence of two major rivers, the **Severn and the Avon**, defines its historic character and its modern-day challenges. The town and surrounding borough are exceptionally vulnerable to flooding, as demonstrated by the severe and widespread floods of 2007. For developers, this presents a complex planning environment where flood risk is the paramount consideration.

Successfully delivering a new development in Tewkesbury requires a sophisticated understanding of this complex river system and the robust planning policies of **Tewkesbury Borough Council**. The council's approach is detailed in its [Strategic Flood Risk Assessment (SFRA)](https://tewkesbury.gov.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/Planning%20policy/Planning%20policy%20evidence%20base/Environment/Strategic%20flood%20risk%20assessment%20-%20level%201/Strategic%20flood%20risk%20assessment%20-%20level%201%20final%20report.pdf) and the [Flood & Water Management Supplementary Planning Document](https://tewkesbury.gov.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/Planning%20policy/Local%20plan/Flood%20and%20water%20management%20SPD%20(February%202018)/Flood%20and%20water%20management%20SPD%20-%20appendices.pdf). ## Tewkesbury's Unparalleled Flood Risk Environment

1.  **The Confluence of Severn and Avon:**
    *   Tewkesbury's primary risk comes from the combined impact of the River Severn and the River Avon. High flows on one or both rivers can cause extensive and deep flooding across the borough. The two rivers have different catchment characteristics, meaning flood peaks can arrive at different times, prolonging the flood event.

2.  **Large and Complex Floodplain:**
    *   The town sits within a wide, flat floodplain. When the rivers overtop their banks, the water can cover vast areas and remain for long periods, impacting infrastructure, property, and agricultural land.

3.  **Critical Infrastructure Vulnerability:**
    *   The 2007 floods highlighted the vulnerability of key infrastructure, including roads, utilities, and emergency services. Any new development must demonstrate it will not be a burden on these services during a flood and can be safely accessed.

4.  **Tewkesbury Borough Council's Stringent Planning Policies:**
    *   Given the high level of risk, the council, as part of the Gloucester, Cheltenham & Tewkesbury [Joint Core Strategy](https://www.tewkesbury.gov.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/Planning%20policy/Planning%20policy%20evidence%20base/Environment/Strategic%20flood%20risk%20assessment%20-%20level%202%20-%20Halcrow%20report%20(October%202011)/Strategic%20flood%20risk%20assessment%20-%20level%202%20executive%20summary.pdf), enforces some of the most rigorous flood risk policies in the country.
    *   A Flood Risk Assessment is not just a formality in Tewkesbury; it is a critical document that will be subject to intense scrutiny. It must prove, unequivocally, that the development will be safe, will not increase risk elsewhere, and incorporates the highest standards of flood resilience and mitigation.
