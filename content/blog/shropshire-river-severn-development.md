Shropshire's stunning rural landscape, historic market towns, and World Heritage Sites are all shaped by the majestic **River Severn**, the UK's longest river. While the Severn is central to the county's identity, it also brings a significant and persistent **flood risk**, creating major challenges for development, infrastructure, and communities from Shrewsbury to Ironbridge and Bridgnorth.

For anyone looking to develop in Shropshire, a deep understanding of the Severn's behaviour and the robust planning framework of **Shropshire Council** is essential. The council's approach is underpinned by a detailed [Level 2 Strategic Flood Risk Assessment (SFRA)](https://shropshire.gov.uk/media/20774/sfra-level-2-report-ev09601.pdf) and the broader [Severn River Basin District Flood Risk Management Plan](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/63750f6de90e0728553b5654/Severn-FRMP-2021-2027.pdf). [Aegaea](https://aegaea.com) provides the specialist flood risk expertise needed to develop successfully and sustainably in this dynamic river catchment.

## Key Flood Risk Challenges in Shropshire

1.  **The Dominance of the River Severn:**
    *   The primary flood risk comes from the Severn itself. The river responds to rainfall across a vast catchment in Wales and the Midlands, leading to high flows and prolonged flooding that can impact towns and agricultural land for extended periods.

2.  **Tributary and "Flash" Flooding:**
    *   Numerous tributaries, such as the Rea Brook and the River Teme, flow into the Severn. These can cause significant "flash" flooding after intense, localised storms, often impacting communities before the main river peak arrives.

3.  **Groundwater and Surface Water Issues:**
    *   Beyond the main rivers, many areas in Shropshire are susceptible to [groundwater flooding](/blog/groundwater-flooding-risk-from-below) and [surface water runoff](/blog/surface-water-flooding-hidden-menace-prepare), particularly where the local geology and drainage systems are unable to cope with heavy rainfall.

4.  **Shropshire Council's Planning Framework:**
    *   Shropshire Council's Local Plan is heavily informed by the SFRA, which identifies functional floodplain and areas at high risk. There is a strong policy presumption against development in these areas.
    *   For any application in a flood risk zone, a site-specific Flood Risk Assessment is mandatory. This must demonstrate that the development will be safe for its lifetime, will not increase flood risk elsewhere, and includes appropriate resilience and mitigation measures. The council's [Preliminary Flood Risk Assessment](https://next.shropshire.gov.uk/media/wtwncmdj/preliminary-risk-assessment.pdf) provides additional context.
